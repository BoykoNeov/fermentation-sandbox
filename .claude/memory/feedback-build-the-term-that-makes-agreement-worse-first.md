When a result is held open by **two omitted terms of opposite sign**, build the one that makes
the headline agree **worse** first. Never the flattering one, and never both in one beat.

**Why:** D-180 predicted 63–92 % of beer's measured pH drop and named both omissions itself:
three falling wort acids (~+0.2–0.3 pH, missing base) and dissolved CO₂ (~−0.3 pH). They
roughly cancel. Building CO₂ alone would have moved the number to 76–104 % — near-exact
agreement, arrived at while an uncorrected error of the same size was still propping it up, and
it would have read as a success. D-181 built the acids instead: nominal fell 63–92 % → 42.7–62.2 %,
the joint band 41–105 % → 8.7–81.4 %, and **the corner that used to reach the measurement no
longer does** — because that reach belonged to the one-sidedness, not to the model. Building
them together would have made neither attributable. With the degrading term shipped first, the
remaining term's future arrival is a real improvement rather than a compensation, and *that*
is the whole payoff of the ordering.

**How to apply:** When a record names its own agreement as a margin
([[feedback-a-margin-is-a-claim-about-what-holds-it-open]]), read off the SIGNS before picking
the next beat, and pick the one that costs you the headline. Pre-register the degraded number
so the loss is expected rather than alarming ([[feedback-pre-register-the-cheap-prediction]]),
and say plainly in the record that the model now agrees less well — hedging it invites someone
to "fix" it back. Corollary: an omitted term whose sign you have not established is not sized,
whatever its magnitude estimate says. See [[project-fermentation-sandbox]] and
[[feedback-closer-to-reality-decides]] — the faithful option ships even when the number gets
worse.
