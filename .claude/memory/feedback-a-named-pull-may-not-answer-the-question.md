When a record says "blocked pending <named paper>", it has made **two** claims: that the source is
unavailable, and that obtaining it would close the question. The second is never checked, because
the first looks like the whole story. At D-199 both were false at once: Nikolantonaki & Waterhouse
2012 had its key figure reprinted with permission in a book on disk since ~50 records earlier —
**and it still could not close the item**, because it measures **nucleophile addition rates**
while the model's dominant quinone consumer (84.78 % of the node) is by construction the fate of
quinone that **no nucleophile captured**. A table of nucleophile rates can have no row for it.

**Why:** the named-pull framing quietly converts an open modelling question into a purchase order,
and a purchase order is not reviewed the way a claim is. So the item stops being examined: nobody
asks "would this paper's *instrument* even measure the quantity we need?" — they just wait. The
real block was structural the entire time, and structural blocks do not get cheaper by waiting.

**How to apply:** before recording, carrying forward, or acting on "blocked pending X", state in
one line **what measurement X reports** and **which quantity in the model it would set**. If those
two do not line up, the block is structural and must be recorded that way — the wanted source
belongs in the record as *supporting evidence*, not as the gate. When a named pull does arrive,
grade it against that line rather than closing the item on arrival; the honest outcome may be
"obtained, and the block is unchanged". Pair this with
[[feedback-check-the-blocker-is-still-blocking]] (the premise may already be demolished) and
[[feedback-paywalled-is-one-host]] (the availability half is usually wrong too) — at D-199 all
three failure modes were present in one item.
