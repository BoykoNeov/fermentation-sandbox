---
name: feedback-an-expectation-transfers-with-the-channel-not-the-subject
description: An expectation derived on one observable, carried to a number reported on another, can be right about the mechanism and wrong about the direction
metadata:
  type: feedback
---

D-234 §9 expected the oxidative cascade wiring to **widen** copper's artefact, reasoning from
D-141: moving `f(Cu)` to the activation node made copper multiply the whole O₂ total instead of
~60 % of one rate. Measured at D-237, the cascade is the **mildest** of the three wirings —
15.38 % against the direct set's 16.65 %.

The reasoning was not careless; it was about the **O₂ channel**, and on that channel it is right
(the cascade moves more than twice the O₂ spread). The band is reported on **browning**, and there
it inverts: copper multiplies one shared gate that every downstream sink splits, so it changes
*when* a fixed O₂ dose is consumed far more than how much becomes pigment. The expectation survived
two records unmeasured because both channels are "oxidation".

**Why:** an expectation carries the name of its subject ("copper's effect"), not the name of the
observable it was derived on. Once written down it looks like a property of the subject, and the
next record inherits it as one.

**How to apply:** When recording an expectation, name the observable it was derived on, not just
the mechanism. When inheriting one, ask what quantity the number you are about to predict is
reported in — if it is not the quantity the reasoning was done in, the expectation is untested and
its **direction** is the part most likely to be wrong.
