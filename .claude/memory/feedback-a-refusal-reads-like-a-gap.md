An item carried in a record's closing "still open" list can be one of two opposite things, and
the list renders them in the same sentence shape: **an opening** (nothing sources it yet, build
it when something does) or **a closing** (it was refused *on the grounds that* the fix would be
a guess). At D-186 both were sitting in the same copied-forward paragraph. The copper-slot
disconnect reads as unsourced and is actually a decision — D-149 refused it on the D-148
precedent — so offering it as "unblocked work" would have described a taken decision as an
absence. D-150's aging-pH item, one line away, was a genuine opening and became that beat.

**Why:** the distinguishing evidence is not age, not how many records repeat it, and not the
wording of the list — it is what the *originating record* says about why it was left. Repetition
is the trap rather than the signal: the same copy-forward tail still names D-143's **withdrawn**
SO2 item and points at "the natural next oxidative beat" that the very next record **refused**
(D-150's pH term). A list that is copied is not re-checked, so it preserves the shape of an open
item long after the content stopped being one.

**How to apply:** before offering anything from a closing list, open the record that *originated*
the item and read the reason. "Nothing sources one" is an opening. "Refused, because the fix would
be a guess" is a closing, and re-proposing it is asking to overturn a decision — which may be
legitimate, but must be *offered as an overturn*, not as available work. The project's tier system
permits a labelled guess, so "we could just tag it speculative" is never by itself a reason the
refusal was wrong. See [[feedback-verify-latest-state-not-breadcrumbs]] for the same failure at
the level of a whole "Next:" list, and [[project-fermentation-sandbox]] for the ledger that is
the authority when the tail disagrees.
